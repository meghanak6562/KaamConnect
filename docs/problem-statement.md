# Problem Statement

## Official Problem Statement

**Problem Statement 4: Hyper-Local Job Matching for Informal & Semi-Skilled Workers**

> Job seekers in non-metropolitan areas often struggle to find nearby employment opportunities because most existing job platforms primarily focus on white-collar professionals.
>
> Informal and semi-skilled workers, such as electricians, plumbers, carpenters, masons, painters, construction workers, and daily-wage labourers, lack access to reliable, location-based job opportunities.
>
> The challenge is to develop a hyper-local, voice-enabled job matching platform that connects informal and semi-skilled workers with nearby employers and small businesses through a simple, accessible digital interface.

---

## Understanding the Gap

### 1. Who Experiences This Problem?

The informal and semi-skilled workforce forms the backbone of local economies in Tier-2, Tier-3, and semi-urban communities. Key worker groups include:

| Worker Category | Typical Work Context | Current Discovery Pain Points |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Electricians** | Residential wiring, appliance repair, shop maintenance | Depend on word-of-mouth or local hardware store referrals; miss nearby urgent jobs. |
| **Plumbers** | Pipe fitting, leak repairs, bathroom installations | No structured way to advertise availability within a 3–10 km radius. |
| **Carpenters** | Furniture repair, door/window fitting, site woodwork | Travel long distances to labour chowks without guaranteed work. |
| **Masons** | Brickwork, plastering, tiling, small renovations | Irregular daily-wage engagement; middlemen often take commissions. |
| **Painters** | Interior/exterior painting, waterproofing | Seasonal demand spikes with poor visibility into local household needs. |
| **Construction Workers** | Site helpers, concrete mixing, loading/unloading | Forced to wait at physical labour stands every morning with zero wage transparency. |
| **Daily-Wage Labourers** | General errands, agricultural support, shifting, cleaning | High literacy barriers on English-first job portals; lack formal resumes. |

### 2. Why Existing Job Platforms Fail

1. **White-Collar Bias**: Mainstream portals (LinkedIn, Naukri, Indeed) require PDF resumes, email addresses, formal degrees, and multi-step applications unsuitable for daily-wage or trade work.
2. **Geographic Coarseness**: Existing platforms filter at the city or district level rather than hyper-local radii (e.g., 2 km, 5 km, 10 km), which is critical when workers commute on foot, bicycle, or local transit.
3. **Language & Literacy Barriers**: Most interfaces are English-only and text-heavy, excluding workers who are more comfortable speaking in Kannada, Hindi, or regional dialects.
4. **Slow Hiring Cycles**: Informal jobs are often urgent (same-day or next-day). Multi-week corporate recruitment pipelines do not work for a shop owner who needs a plumber today.
5. **Broker Dependency**: Without a direct digital matching layer, workers and small businesses rely on informal contractors or labour chowks, reducing take-home wages and trust.

---

## Success Criteria for a Solution

A viable platform addressing Problem Statement 4 must:
- Match workers and employers within a configurable **hyper-local radius** using geographic distance.
- Evaluate **practical trade skills**, **real-time availability**, and **wage expectations** rather than formal resumes.
- Support **voice-first search** and **regional languages** (Kannada, Hindi, English).
- Keep onboarding and job discovery **lightweight and accessible** on low-end smartphones.
